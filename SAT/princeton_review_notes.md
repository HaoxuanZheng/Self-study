# How to use The Princeton Review SAT Premium (2025/2026) — Notes for a 1600 goal

Quick start
- Take the diagnostic test included in the book to establish a baseline and identify priority weaknesses.
- Skim the book's table of contents and note which chapters map to your weak subskills.

Daily workflow (example)
- Day: 90–120 minutes total (see `study_plan.md` for the weekly cadence).
  - Start with targeted review (read the short content summary for the topic).
  - Do the book's practice problems for that topic under timed conditions when appropriate.
  - Record every incorrect or uncertain question in an error log (topic, mistake type, thought process).
  - Re-solve logged problems after reviewing the relevant concept until you can do them unaided.

Weekly workflow
- 3–4 days: focused topic practice + short timed sections.
- 1 day: longer timed sections (one Reading and one Math section) with review.
- 1 day: deeper content review and error-log consolidation.

Using the Princeton Review book effectively
- Treat the book as a structured source of extra practice and strategy—not the final arbiter of official scoring. Prioritize College Board official tests for score calibration.
- When you take a test in the book, simulate test conditions (timed, single-sitting, minimal breaks) and score it against official scoring rules.
- Use the book's explanations to diagnose reasoning errors; if an explanation is unclear, re-solve the problem using a second resource (Khan Academy, UWorld, or PWN).

Specific tips by section
- Reading: annotate passages (main idea, author's tone/purpose, structure). Practice evidence-based questions by underlining the sentence that supports your answer.
- Writing & Language: compile a short list of high-frequency grammar rules (subject-verb agreement, parallelism, pronoun clarity, verb tenses) and drill them daily.
- Math: memorize common formulas, practice algebraic manipulation, and run no-calculator sets frequently to ensure fluency.

Integrations
- Use Khan Academy for official College Board practice problems and personalized recommendations.
- Use UWorld or PWN for additional high-difficulty math and targeted explanations.
